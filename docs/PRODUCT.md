# Product and commercial brief

## Objective

Build an original entertainment channel with a repeatable production process. Near-term goal: discover a format people choose to finish, share and return to. Longer-term goal: contribution profit after production and operating costs. Views, profit and production speed are separate measures; none is a substitute for the others.

The Spark's potential advantage is affordable iteration, local asset reuse and automated production. Programming can create persistent characters, mechanically correct action and a growing library of settings. These advantages become valuable only if the output is enjoyable.

## Initial audience and format hypothesis

Start with visually understandable, mostly wordless, general-audience shorts featuring original recurring characters in unusual but consistent worlds. Use short English packaging initially. This is a testable global distribution hypothesis; do not assume it will reach high-paying countries. India-focused Hindi/local-cultural versions are a later controlled test, not the default chosen solely from population size.

Pixel art is one possible art direction, not a concession to poor quality. Require expressive poses, deliberate lighting, readable movement and polished audio. Do not use Nintendo characters, sprites, sounds, logos or copied game scenes. Treat the working title `Odd Rules` and earlier robot designs as replaceable until the owner selects a look; no trademark availability claim has been made.

Three initial content families within one consistent series:

| Family | Viewer question | Real payoff | What makes episodes distinct |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cooperation puzzle | How can both get through? | A visible solution using an established rule | New obstacle, resource tradeoff and solution |
| Competition with reversal | Who has the better strategy? | Earlier clue changes the outcome | Different strategies and fair rules |
| Small visual mystery | Why does this object behave strangely? | Reveal explains previously visible evidence | A different mechanism and character consequence |

These are creative hypotheses, not researched rankings of the most addictive formats. Public views cannot reveal private rewatch or returning-viewer data.

## Creative constraints for the first series

- Approximately 20–35 seconds, usually 4–7 shots. These are editable design defaults.
- The first shot shows an action, problem or intriguing consequence. Avoid a logo intro or explanatory title card consuming the opening.
- One central question, a readable complication and an actual payoff. A satisfying ending can support replay; do not withhold the promised answer to force replay.
- Early setup establishes any object or rule required for the solution. No random butterfly, disappearing prop or miraculous new ability.
- Characters have different motives and consistent physical traits. Show reactions and decisions, not only objects moving across a stage.
- A loop is optional. Use it only if the ending still resolves the story. Do not make every episode the same hidden cut.
- Sound contributes causality and emotion. Speech is optional; subtitles cannot rescue incomprehensible action.
- Reuse assets and rules; vary the actual story. Seed changes and colour swaps do not count as new episodes.

## Quality decision

Before scaling, the owner watches complete clips at normal phone size with sound and muted. Select a direction that they would willingly show a friend. Review animation polish, staging, surprise, humour and emotional payoff. A technically correct but dull clip fails this gate.

No automatic score can establish shareability. Collect reasons and timecodes when a shot is rejected, so the pipeline improves specific problems.

## Economics

Track marginal cash cost and an optional fully loaded estimate separately:

`cash production cost = measured electricity + API charges + allocated paid assets/storage`

`fully loaded cost = cash production cost + operator hours × chosen hourly value + chosen hardware allocation`

`contribution = actual attributed revenue − cash production/distribution cost`

Record development effort separately from recurring episode production. Include rejected attempts in batch costs. If nothing is accepted, cost per accepted episode is undefined, not zero. Revenue is zero when confirmed absent; unknown when not available. Currency conversion needs an explicit rate and date.

Do not force hardware cost into a paid-vs-local comparison selectively. Compare the same output quality, failure rate and operator effort. Buying an occasional cloud shot may eventually be rational, but paid generation remains disabled until the user authorises it.

YouTube policy distinguishes original contributions from repetitive/mass-produced output; automation does not provide monetisation eligibility. Review the actual channel and current requirements before publishing. [YouTube monetisation policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
